## 1. SAR Strategy B Energy Claim

### 1.1 Inspected Evidence
- **Source File**: `weeks/week_04/models/resnet_meet.py` (lines 31–47)
- **Implementation**:
  ```python
  original_conv = self.backbone.conv1
  # original_weight shape: (64, 3, 7, 7)
  original_weight = original_conv.weight
  # Map channels: preserve VV (ch 0), VH (ch 1), distribute ch 2 evenly
  new_weight = original_weight[:, :2, :, :] + original_weight[:, 2:3, :, :] / 2.0
  self.backbone.conv1.weight.copy_(new_weight)
  ```

### 1.2 Mathematical Derivation
Let $W_{orig} \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 3 \times 7 \times 7}$ represent the pretrained ImageNet conv1 weights with channels $c \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ ($R, G, B$).  
The Strategy B transformation constructs $W_B \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 2 \times 7 \times 7}$ via:
$$W_B[:, 0] = W_{orig}[:, 0] + \frac{1}{2} W_{orig}[:, 2]$$
$$W_B[:, 1] = W_{orig}[:, 1] + \frac{1}{2} W_{orig}[:, 2]$$

#### Linear Weight Sum (Zeroth-Frequency / DC Gain)
Summing across the input channels:
$$\sum_{c=0}^1 W_B[:, c] = W_{orig}[:, 0] + W_{orig}[:, 1] + 2 \times \left(\frac{1}{2} W_{orig}[:, 2]\right) = \sum_{c=0}^2 W_{orig}[:, c]$$
The **linear sum of filter coefficients across channels is 100% conserved**. A spatially uniform input $x_0 = x_1 = x_2 = 1$ yields the identical pre-activation sum before and after adaptation.

#### Quadratic Energy (Frobenius Norm Squared)
Filter energy is defined as the squared Frobenius norm $\|W\|_F^2 = \sum_{i,c,h,w} W_{i,c,h,w}^2$:
$$\|W_B[:, 0]\|_F^2 = \|W_{orig}[:, 0]\|_F^2 + \frac{1}{4} \|W_{orig}[:, 2]\|_F^2 + \langle W_{orig}[:, 0], W_{orig}[:, 2] \rangle$$
$$\|W_B[:, 1]\|_F^2 = \|W_{orig}[:, 1]\|_F^2 + \frac{1}{4} \|W_{orig}[:, 2]\|_F^2 + \langle W_{orig}[:, 1], W_{orig}[:, 2] \rangle$$

Summing both channels:
$$\|W_B\|_F^2 = \|W_{orig}[:, 0]\|_F^2 + \|W_{orig}[:, 1]\|_F^2 + \frac{1}{2} \|W_{orig}[:, 2]\|_F^2 + \langle W_{orig}[:, 0] + W_{orig}[:, 1], W_{orig}[:, 2] \rangle$$

### 1.3 Empirical Measurement on Pretrained Torchvision Weights
Calculated using `ResNet50_Weights.DEFAULT`:
- Original 3-channel Frobenius Norm: $\|W_{orig}\|_F = \mathbf{26.114748}$
- Strategy B 2-channel Frobenius Norm: $\|W_B\|_F = \mathbf{27.756765}$ (Ratio: **1.062877**)
- Original Total Energy: $\|W_{orig}\|_F^2 = \mathbf{681.980103}$
  - Channel 0 (R): $228.171265$ (33.46%)
  - Channel 1 (G): $298.592651$ (43.78%)
  - Channel 2 (B): $155.216171$ (22.76%)
- Strategy B Total Energy: $\|W_B\|_F^2 = \mathbf{770.438232}$ (Ratio: **1.129708**, **+12.97%**)
- Cross-channel Covariance Term: $\langle W_0 + W_1, W_2 \rangle = \mathbf{+166.066162}$

Because ImageNet conv1 filters have positive spatial correlation across color channels, the cross-term is strongly positive.

### 1.4 Section 1 Conclusion
- **Classification**: **INCORRECT (TERMINOLOGY)**  
- **Finding**: Stating that Strategy B *"preserves 100% of pretrained filter energy"* is mathematically false if energy is defined as the $L_2$ / Frobenius norm squared. Total filter energy increases by **+12.97%** due to constructive cross-channel correlation. What Strategy B actually preserves is **100% of the linear filter coefficient sum** ($\sum_c W[:, c]$).
- **Recommended Documentation Qualification**: Update references in reports from *"preserves 100% of pretrained filter energy"* to:  
  > *"Strategy B preserves 100% of the total linear weight sum across channels while redistributing the third channel equally, resulting in a +12.97% increase in Frobenius energy due to positive cross-channel covariance."*

### 2.3 Mathematical Explanation of Ranking Divergence
For unnormalized vectors $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}$:
$$d_{L2}^2(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \|\mathbf{u}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{v}\|^2 - 2 \langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle$$
- In raw ResNet features, vector norms vary widely (SAR norms: [7.99, 17.36]; MS norms: [6.87, 22.53] — a >3-fold variation).
- Under raw $L_2$, a gallery vector $\mathbf{v}$ with a small norm $\|\mathbf{v}\|$ has a small $\|\mathbf{v}\|^2$ penalty and is systematically favored over vectors with larger norms, regardless of angular direction.
- Under L2-normalized inner product, vectors lie on the unit hypersphere ($S^{d-1}$ where $\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}\| = \|\hat{\mathbf{v}}\| = 1$), eliminating magnitude bias:
  $$s_{IP}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}, \hat{\mathbf{v}}) = \cos \theta$$
- **Why aggregate metrics can seem similar**: In zero-shot cross-modal retrieval, both metrics produce near-zero Recall@1 (0.18% vs. 0.22%) because neither metric has aligned the two sensor spaces. In same-modal retrieval, label-overlap masks ranking differences because many gallery items share the same land-cover label. However, the underlying top-10 rankings agree in exact order less than 1% of the time.

### 2.4 Section 2 Conclusion
- **Classification**: **VERIFIED**
- **Finding**: Raw $L_2$ and normalized cosine produce **substantially different rankings** (exact ordered agreement at $K=10$ is $\le 0.82\%$). The identical aggregate numbers in `baseline_results_table.csv` and `map_results.txt` occurred because both originated from the same normalized cosine run, not because raw $L_2$ and cosine are equivalent.
- **Recommended Documentation Qualification**: Formally record that `baseline_results_summary.txt` preserves the historical raw-$L_2$ metrics, while `baseline_results_table.csv`, `map_results.txt`, and `canonical_cosine_baseline_results.txt` document the canonical cosine metrics.
